SD62 district117% today, passing 120% by 2027 and 130% by 2032 · ~400 new students a year · relief (new Langford secondary, 2,000 students) not until Fall 2030 — tap to expand

SD62 already runs at 14,543 students, 117% of operating capacity, with 19 of 26 schools that have capacity figures sitting over capacity. The projection in the SD62 Financial Plan, as summarised by trustee Jeff Bateman — a secondary summary, not a direct quote from the plan, and not a forecast of this page — has utilisation passing 120% by 2027 and 130% by 2032, on roughly 4.3% annual growth, about 400 new students per year, requiring 50+ additional portables.

Inside the district: Millstream Elementary is already at 168% (335 students / 199 operating capacity); Belmont Secondary is at 142% (1,705 / 1,200) and Royal Bay Secondary at 123% (1,725 / 1,400), with the 2027–2030 gap years before the new Langford secondary opens in Fall 2030.

Collingwood Kingsway1,082 homes under review, not approved · nearest elementary closed — tap to expand

At 3410–3436 Kingsway (the Safeway site), a rezoning application proposes 1,082 purpose-built secured rental homes in five towers of 19–29 storeys — as reported by Daily Hive, Nov 19, 2026. The application is under review — not approved. Council voted on Nov 17, 2026 only to expand the consultation list; no public-hearing date has been set.

The nearest elementary, Sir Guy Carleton (3250 Kingsway), is closed — fire in August 2016, and a Vancouver School Board closure bylaw in December 2025.

The finding is an absence: no published catchment-impact modelling exists for this site. Nobody has publicly modelled where roughly 54 students (1,082 high-rise homes at the published 0.05 yield — see the calculator) would go, given that the nearest elementary school is closed. That modelling gap, on a live application of this size, is exactly what a housing → schools forecast exists to close.

Five projection sets & horizons

Before asking what a forecast says, ask which forecast it is. A single Vancouver School Board committee report carries five enrolment projection sets from four producers, produced across a 13-month span — and no record read for this page states which set governs which determination. Every set gets a column naming the determination it governs, and a base-date line per producer. Where the record is silent, the column says so — it is not filled in by guesswork.

The money layer — Richmond & Carleton

Forecasts are not abstract. When a district runs out of room — or holds a building it cannot use — the miss lands in dollars. Two published ledgers, stated exactly as published.

Richmond: a $60 million school site, 90% paid for by selling another school

On 23 June 2025 the Richmond Board of Education announced the purchase of the site and building at 8671 Odlin Crescent (a school opened in 1962) for a new City Centre Elementary, with an opening target as early as September 2026. The enrolment case behind it: enrolment up 42% over four years, in schools built for 1,641 students now serving over 2,000 — at least 359 students over, roughly 122% of the built figure.

A design difference, not a shortfall: Redmond's $7,277 is an amount actually charged per single-family home; the BC figures are ceilings. The charged fee is 7.3× the BC low-density maximum and 12.1× the high-density maximum — that gap describes two differently designed systems, not money BC failed to collect.
The plan comes before the fee. In Washington, an impact fee may only fund system improvements set out in a capital facilities plan (Growth Management Act, RCW 36.70A.070(3)). In BC, an Eligible School Sites Proposal must rest on a district long-term facilities plan of at least 10 years. No plan, no fee — which is why the horizons in the provenance section above matter in dollars, not just in years.

Method note — yields, Baragar & what cannot be forecast yet

Districts do not guess enrolment. They forecast with Baragar Systems cohort-survival modelling — tracking each grade cohort as it moves through the system — combined with local knowledge: municipal development data and yield assumptions about how many students new housing will produce. The Vancouver School Board's June 2025 enrolment update, for example, compares five scenarios and publishes a 2039 utilisation map — inside a board agenda PDF.

Forecast label — End year: 2039 · Producer: Vancouver School Board · Produced: 16 Jun 2025 (Enrolment Update Report 2025)

The yield calculator carries its own label: no end year — it is a scenario tool, not a dated forecast. Its yield rates were produced by the Vancouver School Board on 16 Jun 2025 (Enrolment Update Report 2025, Figure 23).

There is no single provincial yield factor. Districts publish ranges and scenarios, not one number — so every forecast on this page, including everything the calculator produces, is a scenario, not a prediction.

Source hierarchy: official district / ministry / city documents first, then the organisation's own announcements, then news reporting, then secondary summaries — a figure is never presented as stronger than its source.

That is the gap. The modelling exists, but it lives in agenda packages and financial plans. Nobody publishes a live, per-school, housing-driven "tips over in year X" list in one public place. This page is a prototype of that list: real housing pipeline figures, joined to real school capacity figures, run through published yield rates, with every step sourced and dated.

Rules this page follows

  • Every figure carries a source and an as-of date.
  • Proposed is never shown as decided; under review is labelled under review.
  • District projections are labelled as the district's — not presented as this page's forecast.
  • No boundary is drawn, and no catchment asserted, until a district publishes it.

What a scenario is — and is not

  • A scenario applies a published yield rate to a published homes figure. The arithmetic is exact; the outcome is not a prediction.
  • Actual enrolment depends on who moves in, when homes complete, and which catchment a district assigns — none of which a yield rate can know.
  • Scenarios are for asking better questions earlier — while a rezoning is still under review, not after the portables arrive.

What we cannot forecast yet

A forecast needs a housing number. Where no number has been published, this page attaches none — an honest blank, not a guess.

The general rule

  • Approved homes can be run as scenarios with the status stated.
  • Proposed homes are labelled proposed — never counted as decided.
  • Unquantified plans get no number at all. A missing figure is reported as missing, not estimated.
