One region done properly — then the same watch, province by province

Catchment Change Watch is a product, not a one-off report. It starts where a review is live and verifiable, proves the full workflow — timeline, capacity calculator, school briefs, freshness checks, and a plain-language brief people can actually use — and then repeats that workflow in the next province with that province's own sources. No figures are carried across provincial lines.

What travels unchanged: source + as-of date on every figure · proposed never shown as decided · operating vs. nominal capacity always labelled · no boundary drawn until a district publishes it · a freshness audit for every new source. That discipline is the product — the region is just where it is applied.

Method note — sourced and labelled in French from the start

How a new province gets added

  • 1. Source inventory: list every district / authority, its review calendar, and where it publishes capacity and enrolment — before collecting a single number.
  • 2. Definitions first: record how that province defines operating and nominal capacity, so figures are never mixed across systems.
  • 3. Geography: build on Canada's standard geography — dissemination areas from the Census — so school, housing, and population data join cleanly at the same scale.
  • 4. Freshness audit: every source gets an as-of date and a re-check date on day one, not as an afterthought.
  • 5. No invented boundaries: a catchment map appears only when the district itself publishes the boundary.
Coverage order on this page: British Columbia (live) · Alberta · Ontario · Quebec. No enrolment, capacity, or boundary figures are claimed for Alberta, Ontario, or Quebec until they are sourced from those provinces' own publications.

Working calculator — operating vs. nominal capacity

Utilisation depends on which capacity you divide by. Operating capacity is the seats a school is actually staffed and run for; nominal capacity is the building's design capacity. The same enrolment can read "nearly full" one way and "room to spare" the other. Type in any school's three numbers and see both readings at once.

Scenario — how many students could 2,000 homes add?

This is scenario arithmetic, not a forecast and not a district projection. It simply asks: at different student-yield rates per home, how does the added enrolment compare with Queen Alexandra's 120 spare seats? A real brief would replace these rates with the district's own yield factors.

Even the middle scenario turns a 54%-full school into an over-capacity one — if those students land in this catchment, which is precisely the catchment-impact question above that has not been modelled publicly. That is the finding: the decision hinges on numbers nobody has published yet.

Questions a board-ready brief must answer: What does the fix cost, and who pays? What yield factor does the district use for tower homes? Which neighbouring schools absorb overflow? And what, concretely, is being proposed — upgrade, replace, expand, or re-catchment?

A second housing-without-schools case, noted briefly: Collingwood / Thompson — 1,082 rental homes (~400 family-sized) and a $95M seismic estimate for Thompson with no starting year published — again with no nearby-school capacity or enrolment forecast attached. Same gap, same fix: join the housing figures to the school figures, per catchment, with the method stated.

Data notes — freshness & source checks

Public school pages and listings were checked the way a data pipeline should be: does the number on screen still match its source, and is the source's age visible? Notes below, each with the check that resolves it.

● Check — Stale data?
SD61 facility condition
45 of 46 Victoria schools rated "Poor or worse"; 12 flagged on the pages themselves as possibly upgraded / out of date.
Check: Reconcile each rating against the latest district / ministry reporting, stamp "as of," re-check oldest first.
● Check — Missing data
Capacity coverage
Only 951 of 1,569 public schools have capacity figures; independent schools have none — yet utilisation is a headline measure.
Check: Coverage list by district, backfill queue ranked by how often each school is viewed.
● Check — Provenance
Source line not visible
No source / "as of" line was visible on the school pages as fetched in text form. It may render in the live app — that needs an in-browser check before calling it a defect.
Check: Verify live, then render source + as-of from the underlying record so it cannot drift from the figure.
● Check — Summary accuracy
Generated summaries
One school's IB summary text names a different school; a districts index reads "Powell RIver." Small errors, but they signal unreviewed generated text.
Check: Entity-match validation (the school named in the text must be the school on the page) plus a human review queue.
● Check — Old sources, current voice
Public posts citing older plans
Public material still in circulation cites a June 16, 2025 capital plan (seismic), June 2025 enrolment material, and a Dec 30, 2021 plan (a 76% utilisation figure), with no note on whether the figures have since changed or been funded.
Check: Put a source-age stamp on every cited figure, wherever it is republished, with a re-check date.
● Opportunity
Healthcare & walkability
Families and realtors consistently ask for two things school data alone does not answer: how close is healthcare, and what is actually walkable — coffee, bank, restaurant — from a given school or home.
Next step: A clinics + walk-time layer per school — sourced, dated, and kept separate from school data.

How to use this watch

Check the watchlist before you rely on a catchment: if a school is marked proposed / under review, today's boundary is not a guarantee. Use the calculator when a district quotes a utilisation percentage — ask which capacity it was divided by. Use the territory brief when you need the short version for a client, a move, or a board meeting.

Method, in one paragraph: every figure was taken from a published source and dated; conflicting or stale figures are flagged, not smoothed over; statuses follow the district's own labels (proposed vs. decided); and anything a source has not published — a boundary map, a cost, a forecast — is left blank and named as missing, rather than filled in.

For families

  • See which schools are over capacity and which catchments are under review, with the decision date.
  • Know the consultation window while you can still be heard — not after the decision.

For realtors & planners

  • Give clients a sourced, dated brief instead of a guess about a future catchment.
  • Track housing growth against school capacity, per catchment, with the method stated.
