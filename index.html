Case 1 — SD62 Sooke: a district growing past its own plan

SD62 already runs at 14,543 students, 117% of operating capacity, with 19 of 26 schools that have capacity figures sitting over capacity. The district's own projection — labelled here as a district projection, not a forecast of this page — has utilisation passing 120% by 2027 and 130% by 2032, on roughly 4.3% annual growth, about 400 new students per year, requiring 50+ additional portables.

The housing behind it:

  • Royal Bay, Colwood — master-planned community of 3,400 residences for 10,000 people, with two schools, and a 7-acre site reserved for a future 500-student elementary.
  • Royal Beach, Colwood — 2,850 homes, a $1.2B, 15-year buildout.
  • Langford — built 5,062 new homes between 2016 and 2021; population grew 31.8% over the same census period.
Reading: the growth is not a surprise arriving later — the homes are already approved and building. The district projection says the system passes 120% in 2027. The open question is which individual schools tip first, and that is a per-school, housing-by-housing question — see Cases 2 and 3.

Both Westshore secondaries are well over capacity today. The relief is real and approved — a new 2,000-student Langford secondary — but it does not open until Fall 2030. Between now and then, the district projection has SD62 passing 120% utilisation in 2027 and continuing to grow at roughly 400 students a year. The forecast is the gap: 2027–2030, four school years of growth with no new secondary seats.

Queen Alexandra is the opposite problem: a school at 54% of capacity, seismic risk H1, with the district forecasting enrolment to fall to 112 by 2031. Around it, major housing is moving: 1,044 rental homes approved at 1780 E Broadway, a VCC Broadway campus redevelopment proposing 3,300+ homes (announced July 24, 2023 — not yet rezoned or approved), and the Broadway Subway opening in 2027.

Catchment not verified — stated plainly: this page does not assert that the homes above fall inside Queen Alexandra's catchment. No published source checked for this page assigns them to it. What the calculator below shows is only a labelled scenario: if homes of that scale landed in a catchment like this one, at the published yield rate, this is the enrolment they would add.
  • Scenario: 1,044 high-rise homes × 0.05 students per unit ≈ 52 students — against 120 spare seats, absorbable on its own.
  • Scenario: 3,300 high-rise homes × 0.05 ≈ 165 students — more than the school's 120 spare seats, if they were all in this catchment, which is not established.

Case 5 — Collingwood / Kingsway, Vancouver: 1,082 homes, and the nearest elementary is closed

At 3410–3436 Kingsway (the Safeway site), a rezoning application proposes 1,082 purpose-built secured rental homes in five towers of 19–29 storeys. The application is under review — not approved. Council voted on Nov 17, 2026 only to expand the consultation list; no public-hearing date has been set.

The nearest elementary, Sir Guy Carleton (3250 Kingsway), is closed — fire in August 2016, and a Vancouver School Board closure bylaw in December 2025.

The finding is an absence: no published catchment-impact modelling exists for this site. Nobody has publicly modelled where roughly 54 students (1,082 high-rise homes at the published 0.05 yield — see the calculator) would go, given that the nearest elementary school is closed. That modelling gap, on a live application of this size, is exactly what a housing → schools forecast exists to close.

The yield calculator — homes in, students out

Districts turn new housing into expected students with a yield rate: students per home, by housing type. Pick a housing type and a number of homes, set a school's enrolment and operating capacity, and see the students added, the new enrolment, the new utilisation — and whether the school tips over 100%. The result updates as you type.

There is no single provincial yield factor. Districts publish ranges and scenarios, not one number — so every forecast on this page, including everything this calculator produces, is a scenario, not a prediction.

What we cannot forecast yet

A forecast needs a housing number. Where no number has been published, this page attaches none — an honest blank, not a guess.

  • The 18-storey tower proposed at 3468 Tillicum Road has an unspecified rental count (Saanich first reading Nov 23, 2026).
  • So no student number is attached to Tillicum on this page. When a unit count is published, the calculator above can run it the same day.
Sources: Tillicum Burnside Plan materials · District of Saanich council materials, first reading Nov 23, 2026 · Checked Nov 22, 2026

The general rule

  • Approved homes can be run as scenarios with the status stated.
  • Proposed homes are labelled proposed — never counted as decided.
  • Unquantified plans get no number at all. A missing figure is reported as missing, not estimated.

Method — how districts forecast, and the gap this page fills

Districts do not guess enrolment. They forecast with Baragar Systems cohort-survival modelling — tracking each grade cohort as it moves through the system — combined with local knowledge: municipal development data and yield assumptions about how many students new housing will produce. The Vancouver School Board's June 2025 enrolment update, for example, compares five scenarios and publishes a 2039 utilisation map — inside a board agenda PDF.

That is the gap. The modelling exists, but it lives in agenda packages and financial plans. Nobody publishes a live, per-school, housing-driven "tips over in year X" list in one public place. This page is a prototype of that list: real housing pipeline figures, joined to real school capacity figures, run through published yield rates, with every step sourced and dated.

Rules this page follows

  • Every figure carries a source and an as-of date.
  • Proposed is never shown as decided; under review is labelled under review.
  • District projections are labelled as the district's — not presented as this page's forecast.
  • No boundary is drawn, and no catchment asserted, until a district publishes it.

What a scenario is — and is not

  • A scenario applies a published yield rate to a published homes figure. The arithmetic is exact; the outcome is not a prediction.
  • Actual enrolment depends on who moves in, when homes complete, and which catchment a district assigns — none of which a yield rate can know.
  • Scenarios are for asking better questions earlier — while a rezoning is still under review, not after the portables arrive.
